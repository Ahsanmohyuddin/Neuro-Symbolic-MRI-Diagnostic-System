Understanding Alzheimer's Disease

In-depth explanation of all 4 classification stages — what they mean, how they progress, and what you can do.

What Is Alzheimer's Disease?

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurological disorder that causes brain cells to degenerate and die. It is the most common cause of dementia, affecting memory, thinking, behaviour, and the ability to perform everyday activities. The disease is characterized by the abnormal buildup of proteins in and around brain cells — specifically amyloid plaques and tau tangles.

How It Progresses

  • Starts with microscopic protein changes
  • Gradually destroys memory networks
  • Spreads to reasoning and language centers
  • Eventually affects all brain functions

Risk Factors

  • Age (risk doubles every 5 years after 65)
  • Family history and APOE-e4 gene
  • Cardiovascular disease, diabetes
  • Poor sleep, chronic stress, inactivity

Why Early Detection Matters

  • Interventions are most effective early
  • Lifestyle changes can slow progression
  • Medications work best at mild stages
  • Allows time for care planning

The 4 Classification Stages

Risk Score: 8 / 100

1. NonDemented — Safe Zone

🟢 No Signs Detected

This classification indicates no detectable signs of Alzheimer's-related changes in the brain scan. The hippocampus appears normal in size, and cortical thickness is within healthy range. While this is excellent news, it is not a guarantee against future development — especially for individuals with genetic risk factors.

Brain Changes

  • Normal hippocampal volume
  • Healthy cortical thickness
  • No visible plaque accumulation
  • Normal ventricular size

Nutritional Advice

  • Mediterranean diet (fish, greens, olive oil)
  • Blueberries, walnuts, dark chocolate
  • Minimum 8 glasses of water daily
  • Reduce processed food and sugar

Preventive Lifestyle

  • 30-min aerobic exercise daily
  • Mental exercises: puzzles, reading
  • 7–9 hours quality sleep nightly
  • Regular social interaction
Risk Score: 28 / 100

2. VeryMildDemented — Early Stage

🟡 Act Now

The earliest detectable stage of Alzheimer's disease. Minimal cognitive symptoms are present, often noticed only by the patient. The brain scan may show subtle hippocampal shrinkage and the beginning of amyloid-beta plaque accumulation. This is the most treatable stage — intervention here yields the best outcomes.

Brain Changes

  • Slight hippocampal volume reduction
  • Early amyloid plaque formation
  • Subtle entorhinal cortex thinning
  • Minimal tau tangle accumulation

Nutritional Advice

  • Increase omega-3: salmon, flaxseed
  • Turmeric (curcumin) — anti-inflammatory
  • Green tea and antioxidant-rich foods
  • Reduce red meat and dairy fat

Interventions

  • Neurologist consultation in 2–4 weeks
  • Start cognitive training programs
  • Daily journaling to track changes
  • Memory support groups
Risk Score: 58 / 100

3. MildDemented — Warning Stage

🟠 Seek Help

Cognitive decline is now noticeable and beginning to affect daily activities. Significant amyloid plaque and tau tangle buildup is present in the hippocampus and surrounding cortex. Memory loss, personality changes, and functional difficulties are common. Medical intervention and family support are urgently required.

Brain Changes

  • Significant hippocampal atrophy
  • Progressive neuronal death
  • Cortical thinning spreading outward
  • Enlarged ventricles visible

Nutritional Advice

  • Strict Mediterranean diet
  • Avoid alcohol completely
  • High antioxidant: berries, spinach
  • Small, frequent, balanced meals

Urgent Actions

  • Immediate neurologist consultation
  • Establish medication reminders
  • Do not drive without clearance
  • Appoint a health coordinator
Risk Score: 82 / 100

4. ModerateDemented — Critical Stage

🔴 Urgent Care Required

Advanced Alzheimer's disease with widespread brain changes. Extensive neuronal death across multiple brain regions, including the frontal and temporal lobes. The patient may not recognize family members and is unable to perform daily tasks independently. Full-time specialist care and advanced care planning are essential.

Brain Changes

  • Widespread cortical atrophy
  • Advanced amyloid and tau pathology
  • Severely enlarged ventricles
  • Multiple brain regions affected

Nutritional Advice

  • Soft, easy-to-swallow foods
  • High-nutrition, closely monitored meals
  • Adequate hydration (dehydration risk)
  • Vitamin D, B12 supplementation

Care Plan

  • 24/7 supervised care required
  • Install safety measures at home
  • Establish power of attorney
  • Music therapy and sensory stimulation
